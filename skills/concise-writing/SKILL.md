# Concise Writing

Write for a reader who has only the final text. Remove conversation history, planning rationale, and references to unstated context.

Use the installed `unslop` skill as the baseline. Apply these rules:

1. Lead with the fact, behavior, or action.
2. Keep only details needed to understand or use the subject.
3. Remove throat-clearing, summaries of the writing process, and issue-specific history.
4. Replace vague claims with names, values, commands, or observable behavior.
5. Prefer active voice and short sentences.
6. Remove repeated conclusions, decorative emphasis, and filler transitions.
7. Avoid em dashes, rhetorical questions, binary contrasts, and scripted enthusiasm.
8. Preserve constraints, failure behavior, examples, and other details that prevent misuse.

Before finishing, check each sentence:

- Does it depend on a conversation the reader cannot see?
- Does it explain why the text exists instead of the subject itself?
- Can it lose words without losing meaning?
- Does it repeat a nearby sentence?
- Does it use a concrete term where a broad label would hide behavior?

Revise any sentence that fails this check. Do not shorten code, commands, identifiers, or required legal text.
